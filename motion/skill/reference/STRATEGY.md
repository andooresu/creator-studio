# Ad strategy & concept method

Source of the thinking: Roy H. Williams, *The Wizard of Ads*, distilled and rewritten in our own words for 15–30 s social video ads (the book was written for radio, so "sound" is read here as VO and audio-first Reels). The user's own method will be layered on top as they teach it. Status: **approved by the user** (2026-10-12).

Use this in INTERVIEW round 1 (concept, sales angle, target emotion, expected result) and to self-check every concept before showing it.

## Principles (what we believe)
1. **Feeling decides, facts justify.** Choose ONE target feeling first; facts and proof come after, to back it up.
2. **She is the protagonist, not the brand.** Her world, "tú", present tense. Studio history, awards and equipment only as proof, never as the story.
3. **She goes only where her imagination has already been.** Show the arrival, the chair, the reveal, the mirror, so booking feels like a step she has already taken.
4. **Seduce, don't shout.** Win attention with a thought more interesting than the one she is having. Loud graphics without an idea are a cheap trick.
5. **The predictable is invisible.** Put at least one unexpected word, image or claim in every ad.
6. **The message matters more than the targeting.** Most failed ads failed on what they said.
7. **Plain, specific, brave truth.** A sharp line beats a line softened until nobody could object. Pay for the sharp line with proof.
8. **Prove before she asks.** Name her main doubt and close it on screen: a real result, a number, the process, a guarantee.
9. **One point per ad, many ads per campaign.** Variety lives in the series, not inside one Reel.
10. **Repetition plants, sleep consolidates.** Keep one core message for months and say it better each time; don't replace it. Results build slowly.
11. **Sound carries memory.** The VO and a recurring audio signature (same voice, sonic logo, a repeated phrase) do the remembering. On-screen text supports them.
12. **Hook new to known.** Anchor in moments she recognises: the morning rush, the meeting, the photo, the mirror.
13. **Sell to who she sees herself as.** Give her a line that says who she is.
14. **Feature → "which means…" → feeling.** E.g. lip pigmentation → defined lips on waking → one less thing in her morning → calm.
15. **Delight beats "fair".** "Good price, good service" moves no one. The unexpected extra does, so show it.
16. **Get to the point in seconds.** In factual mode, lead with the payoff and then prove it; no warm-up.
17. **Discounts are a drug.** Use offers rarely, and only when they reinforce what the brand stands for.
18. **Polish is not persuasion.** It has to be believable and memorable, and sound like one person talking to another. Premium visuals raise the price she expects without ever stating it.

## Concept method (run it before any shotlist)
1. **Reason to exist:** why this brand over the others, in one sentence. *Check:* could a competitor say it? If yes, rewrite it.
2. **Insight:** what she asks for vs. what she really wants. ("Microblading" → "that people tell me I look rested.")
3. **Angle:** **emotional** (she lives the experience) or **factual** (payoff first, then fast proof). One mode, one point.
4. **Emotion:** a single word (relief, being seen, control, quiet pride, belonging).
5. **Hook (0–2 s):** inside her world, with something unexpected, without shouting.
6. **Structure for 15–30 s:**
   - 0–2 s: hook
   - 2–8 s: a moment she recognises, the tension
   - 8–20 s: the mental movie of the experience and result, told as "which means" benefits
   - 20–25 s: proof that closes her doubt
   - last 3–5 s: brand signature + CTA
   - It must read with the sound off; with the sound on, the VO is the backbone.
7. **Proof:** her top doubt, answered visibly.
8. **CTA:** describe the next step concretely ("Escríbenos y te respondemos con tu valoración"), not just "llama ya".
9. **Campaign fit:** does it repeat the brand's word, feeling and audio signature? Is it one more episode of a series?

Present each proposal as: **concept · sales angle · emotion · hook line (exact) · beats · proof · CTA · expected result**, plus the checklist score.

## Hook rules
- **Make her curious:** a curious observation, a contradiction, a concrete detail ("Son las 6:40…").
- **Put her inside the scene** in the first line, with "tú".
- **Say something she already feels is true.** Agreeing at the start earns belief for the rest.
- **Make it unfinishable:** if she could complete the line, replace it.
- **Use visual words:** active verbs, concrete nouns, sensory detail (texture, light, sound).

## Copy / VO rules
- **Banned ad-speak:** "calidad y precio", "los mejores", "atención personalizada", "profesionales expertos", "te esperamos", "no te lo pierdas", and any claim without proof.
- **"Tú" far more than the brand name.**
- **No untranslated jargon.**
- **Uneven rhythm**, not predictable parallel lines.
- **Close each line on its strongest image.**
- **Positive framing:** she feels good about who she already is; don't lecture her about what's wrong.
- **Emotional exaggeration is fine; deception is not.**
- **The ad has to match** what staff say on WhatsApp and in the chair.

## Brand vs. sales
- **Two jobs, kept separate:** memory ads (she remembers you when the need appears) and traffic ads (immediate offer). Traffic ads are forgotten when the offer ends.
- **Small budget:** high frequency to a tight local audience rather than spreading thin.
- **Rough split for a small local brand:** ~70–80% consistent brand-memory Reels, 20–30% offer Reels. Every offer still carries the core message. (Our extrapolation, not the book's.)
- **Own a word or feeling + an audio signature,** and keep them across every ad for the brand. Record them in that brand's memory.
- **Judge by bookings and recall,** not likes and comments.

## Red flags (reject or rework)
- The brand is the protagonist.
- A warm-up before the point.
- Noise with no idea behind it.
- A string of clichés.
- Three ideas in one Reel.
- Discount or fake urgency as the engine.
- A new core message every month.
- Beautiful but says nothing.
- Softened by committee.
- Her doubt left open, or the next step unclear.

## Scoring checklist (1–5 each; show it with every proposal; aim ≥ 40/50)
1. She is the protagonist.
2. The 2-second hook is more interesting than her current thought, without shouting.
3. One point, one named emotion.
4. At least one unexpected element.
5. Features turned into felt benefits.
6. Her main doubt closed with proof.
7. She can picture herself living it.
8. Sounds like a person, not like an ad.
9. Concrete CTA.
10. Carries the brand's word, feeling and audio signature, and fits a series.
